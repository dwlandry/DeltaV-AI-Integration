Suppressing alarms

Generally, you respond immediately to alarms. However, occasionally you might find that you are distracted by a nuisance alarm that you cannot immediately respond to. When this occurs, you can temporarily shelve the alarm or permanently remove it from service. Both types of suppression move the alarm from an alarm banner and alarm list to the Alarm Suppress display.

Shelving an alarm typically falls under the control of the operator and is done for the purpose of temporarily suppressing the alarm. When you are ready to respond to it, unshelve the alarm in the Alarm Suppress display and respond to it. If ignored too long or forgotten, the alarm is automatically unshelved when the shelving timer runs out.

Removing an alarm from service falls under the control of maintenance and is used for the purpose of test or repair activities. An out of service alarm remains out of service until it is manually restored to service. In other words, a suppression timer cannot be configured on an out of service alarm.

Note:

  • The shelving timer is configured in the Alarm Properties dialog (Advanced tab).

  • In the default DeltaV configuration, the user must have the Restricted Control key (assigned in the DeltaV User Manager) to suppress process alarms.

  • The procedure for suppressing alarms depends on the type of alarm: a process alarm, a device alarm, an external asset optimization alarm.

The Alarm Suppress display

When suppressed alarms exist, you can open the Alarm Suppress display from a layout's menu bar or the Alarm Filter display. The Alarm Suppress display shows up to 1500 suppressed alarms in the areas within the current user's span of control. The alarms are listed by Module, Parameter, Description, Area, Unit, and Time In.

From this display, you can view suppressed alarms, both shelved and out of service, and you can unsuppress alarms by either unshelving or restoring them to service. You cannot, however, suppress alarms from this display. Instead, use the affected module's detail display to suppress the alarm.

To view suppressed alarms by area, click to open the Alarm Filter display.
Note: Downloading a controller clears, from the Alarm Suppress display, any alarms that were generated from that controller. Those alarms reappear in the alarm list as Active/Unacknowledged Alarms with a new timestamp.

Suppressing process alarms

To suppress a process alarm, open the detail display for the module in which the alarm occurred. In the detail display's Alarms area, next to the alarm you want to suppress, click the check box next to Shlv to shelve the alarm or next to OOS to remove it from service. The suppressed alarm is added to the Alarm Suppress display and removed from the alarm banner and alarm list.

To unsuppress a process alarm (either unshelve it or restore it to service), clear the check mark on the detail display or use the Alarm Suppress display to clear it.

Suppressing device alarms

For devices that support PlantWeb alerts, each alarm (COMM_ALM, FAILED_ALM, MAINT_ALM, and ADVISE_ALM) may be activated by more than one condition, depending on the device. The DeltaV system provides two options for suppressing device alarms:

  • Suppress the alarm through the device alarm's faceplate.

  • Suppress the conditions that can produce alarms.

Suppressing alarms from the faceplate is a user-interface setting that prevents reported alarm conditions from appearing in an alarm banner and alarm list. Suppressing alarm conditions is a device-level setting configured in the Status/Conditions dialog. This type of suppression prevents the device from reporting the associated alarm condition to the DeltaV system. Because alarms contain multiple conditions, suppressions at the alarm level always override suppressions at the condition level.

The following example illustrates how alarm or condition suppression might be used:

A fieldbus device reports that it is due to be calibrated. The calibration due condition appears as a Maintenance alarm in the Conditions dialog, and also in the DeltaV Alarm Banner and Event Chronicle. Because regular service is scheduled for the following week, the user might choose to suppress all Maintenance alarms from that device using the device faceplate. The alarm clears from the Alarm Banner and the Event Chronicle records that the alarm was suppressed. The device does not generate a maintenance alarm until the alarm is unsuppressed.

Alternatively, the user could choose to suppress only the calibration due condition by checking the box above the condition in that device's Conditions dialog. If this was the only condition that caused the maintenance alarm, the alarm clears from the Alarm Banner and the Event Chronicle. However, if another condition in the maintenance alarm activates, a new maintenance alarm is generated. The device will not generate a maintenance alarm because of the calibration due condition until the condition is unsuppressed.

In both cases, the Status/Conditions dialog continues to display the condition as active.

The following image shows a typical device alarm faceplate. From the faceplate, you can suppress and unsuppress each alarm for the device individually. In the default DeltaV configuration, the user must have the Restricted Control key to suppress device alarms.

Active Alarms

Lists the suppressed alarms. Suppressing an alarm prevents activation of the alarm for all related device conditions. You can suppress acknowledged alarms as long as they are active. When you unsuppress an alarm, it is automatically acknowledged.

Suppressing device conditions

You can view and suppress individual conditions at the device level for devices that support PlantWeb alerts. To view and suppress these conditions, click the detail button on the faceplate. Or, select the device in DeltaV Explorer and right-click Status/Conditions. In the default DeltaV configuration, the user must have the Can Calibrate key (assigned in the DeltaV User Manager application) to suppress device conditions.

Suppressing external asset optimization alarms

External asset optimization alarms can be suppressed through the asset alarm's faceplate. The following image shows a typical asset alarm faceplate. From the faceplate, you can suppress and unsuppress each alarm. In the default DeltaV configuration, the user must have the Restricted Control key to suppress asset optimization alarms.
