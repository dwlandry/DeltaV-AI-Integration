Partitioning units

An integral part of your control strategy is deciding what pieces of equipment make up a unit. A unit is a major piece of equipment (in an equipment train or process cell) that performs a specific task. It consists of all of the equipment and control modules that are needed to perform this task. Your unit design is important in that phases control the equipment that makes up a unit. Note that units:

  • Only run one batch at a time.

  • Must operate independently.

The following figure illustrates how the example MIXER and RAW units can be partitioned. The mixer contains the equipment and control modules, such as the agitator, that operate the mixer. In this case, the control modules that make up the agitator should be defined as part of the MIXER unit. However, it might be unclear to which unit certain equipment belongs, such as the valve connecting the RAW and MIXER units. In this example, the valve is included in the MIXER unit.
